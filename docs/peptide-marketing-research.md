# Peptide Clinic: Marketing Do's and Don'ts

For Jess at Perspective Health Iowa, from NuVue. October 2026. This is not legal advice, so have an attorney look at the page before it goes live.

**Heads up:** the rules may loosen soon. A federal plan that would let pharmacies make BPC-157 and similar peptides went to the White House on Oct 15-16 ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/trump-administration-advances-plan-peptide-drugs-favored-rfk-jr-rcna603867)). It isn't final. Everything below reflects today's rules.

## Do

- Sell the care, not the product: Stacey's evaluation, labs, follow-ups, and the convenience of virtual visits.
- Use soft language: "may support," "when appropriate," "results vary."
- Add this line wherever compounded peptides come up: *"Compounded medications are not FDA-approved."*
- Tell patients they must be physically in Iowa for each video visit.
- Get signed telehealth consent and review records before the first visit. Iowa requires both.

## Don't

- **Don't name or price these yet:** BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, AOD-9604, GHK-Cu injections, Melanotan II, and similar. Pharmacies can't legally make them right now, and FDA is sending warning letters about them ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks)).
- Don't say a peptide treats, cures, or heals anything, and don't say "clinically proven."
- Don't call compounded peptides "FDA-approved" or "the same as" a brand drug.
- Don't use before/after photos as proof that peptides work.
- Don't run Google or Facebook ads for peptides yet. Both require LegitScript certification first, which costs about $3,000 in the first year ([Google](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031), [Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/)).

## OK to name, carefully

- **Sermorelin**, with the "not FDA-approved" line.
- **FDA-approved brand drugs** such as Wegovy, Zepbound, or Egrifta, but only if patients actually get the brand product.

## Getting found on Google

- Launch one page, "Peptide Therapy," aimed at searches like *peptide therapy Des Moines* and *peptide clinic Iowa*. A draft is ready and names no specific peptides.
- Add "Peptide Therapy" back to the Google Business Profile once the page is live.
- Ask happy patients for Google reviews.
- Write blog posts that answer common questions, like "Are peptides FDA-approved?" and "What to expect at a virtual peptide visit."
- Most local clinics lead with weight-loss shots. None clearly offers virtual peptide care in Iowa, so that's our angle.

## Questions for Jess

1. Which peptides will Stacey prescribe?
2. Which pharmacy will fill them?
3. How should patients book: a link, phone, or both? Can new patients book a virtual visit directly?
4. What's the pricing, and should any of it go on the page?
5. Can we list Stacey as "Stacey Darnold, ARNP, FNP-C"? ARNP is Iowa's licensed title.
6. Where will virtual patients get their labs drawn?
7. Do you want to pay for LegitScript so we can run ads, or stick with Google search and reviews for now?
8. Can we get a photo of Stacey or the clinic for the top of the page?
